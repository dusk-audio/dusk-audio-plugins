# DuskVerb 2, next release

Not released yet. The version number is set when this release is made.

- Small Drum Room is rebuilt around its reference's early response. The reference puts 89% of an impulse's energy in a group of reflections between 5 and 17 ms, with faint repeats near 70, 100 and 155 ms; the preset now places its hit there too, where it used to spread 39% of the energy between 50 and 150 ms and sound like a room behind the drum. In tone it has 2 to 4 dB more between 1.5 and 8 kHz (its Hi Cut now starts at 6.0 kHz, where it started at 3.6 kHz), less around 125 Hz, and it rolls off above 14 kHz, where the reference ends at 15 kHz and the preset used to run on to the top of the band. Its mid decay multiplier is 1.40, where it was 1.22, and its Width 105%, where it was 114%. With both playing equally loud, every third octave from 50 Hz to 20 kHz is within 3 dB of the reference on a snare and on music; 12 and 10 of the 27 were outside. Measured against the reference, the attack time, the onset slope, the clarity of a snare, the share of energy in the first 50 ms and the prominence of the reflection near 68 ms are now inside their limits; they were outside. The wet level is 1.1 dB lower. The preset costs the same processing within measurement (5.6% of a 128-frame callback at 48 kHz on the development machine, against 5.7%).
- Vocal Plate's low end decays as the reference's does. Its 63 and 125 Hz octaves died away 28% and 23% sooner than the reference's, and half a second after a hit its 40 to 200 Hz tail sat 20 dB or more under it; the low octaves now decay within 10% of the reference's. Its early reflections no longer carry the lows ahead of the tail: the snare and the music were 5 to 7 dB too heavy around 100 Hz. Processing cost is within 0.1% of the previous release's.
- Large Chamber no longer leaves a gap after its first reflections. Its tail used to be held back and ducked after a hit, which left the response up to 17 dB under its reference between 60 and 210 ms and brought the tail in at 220 ms, 4 dB over it; a snare sounded like a slap followed by a late bloom. The early reflections and the tail now follow the reference's level within 2 dB from the first reflection to 3 s, and half of the response's energy arrives by 115 ms (reference 111 ms). The early reflections are brighter and are laid out as the reference's are, between 7 and 73 ms with their strongest about 20 ms in. The tail keeps its top octave: what reaches the reverb tank no longer passes ten modulated filters that together took 7 dB off at 12.7 kHz and 17 dB at 16 kHz, so 200 to 800 ms into the response the tail is 3 dB under the reference at 12.7 kHz and 11 dB at 16 kHz, where it was 6 and 18 dB under. Lo Cut goes from 32 to 28 Hz, and sustained notes around 1 kHz are no longer turned down by up to 6 dB. The low body of a drum holds on as the reference's does: the 125 and 250 Hz part of a snare's tail used to fall 6 dB 17% and 14% sooner than the reference's, and now does so within 6% and 9%. The low end no longer rings on after the music stops: 3.5 to 6 s after a phrase the lows stood 3.8 dB higher against the body than in the reference, and now stand 2.3 dB higher. Processing cost is unchanged (12.5% against 12.7% on the development machine).
- Live Room is rebuilt around its reference's opening. The reference puts 64% of its energy in a bright group of reflections between 20 and 50 ms, the left ones in one polarity and the right ones in the other, so its lows are in opposite phase for the first 200 ms and uncorrelated after; the preset used to have 47% of its energy there, with 9 to 12 dB too much below 300 Hz, 6 to 13 dB too little above 4 kHz, and a tail 4 to 8 dB over the reference. It now has 62% there, every octave of the opening within 2 dB, the lows in opposite phase through the first 200 ms (correlation -0.86 below 300 Hz in the first 60 ms, as the reference) and not in the tail (-0.24). Width is 100% (it was 150%: the preset's own stages now set the width of each band), Lo Cut 10 Hz, the tail's top octave is 9 dB up at 14 kHz, and sustained notes are no longer turned down: a held 1 kHz note used to be cut by up to 20 dB and lows by up to 7 dB. In mono the reverb's lows largely cancel, as the reference's do. Processing cost is lower (7.0% against 7.6% on the development machine).
- Nonlinear engine: Decay sets the length of the burst up to 2.5 s. In 1.0.1 the burst stopped getting longer at 783 ms while the Decay control and its display went on up to 30 s, so the burst seemed to be cut off at about 0.8 s. Above 2.5 s the burst stays at 2.5 s, and the decay readout and the host's parameter display show 2.50 s (an LV2 host's own display shows the knob position from 0 to 1, not a time). Settings up to 783 ms sound exactly as before.
- A project saved with 1.0.1 on the Nonlinear engine with Decay above 783 ms played that 783 ms burst and now plays the Decay it shows; set Decay to 783 ms to get the old length back (when typing it, include the unit). Recorded Decay automation above 783 ms likewise now plays what it says.
- The longer bursts cost more. Measured on the development machine, the Nonlinear engine at Decay 2.5 s needs about 3 times the processing of the 1.0.1 burst at its longest. While Decay is moving it needs about 1.8 times its own cost at rest, because the old and the new burst play together while each change settles, and for the 50 ms of a crossfade between two Nonlinear presets both bursts play. Settings up to 783 ms cost what they did. Every instance, whichever engine it plays, now reserves about 8.7 MB for the Nonlinear engine at 48 kHz and 33 MB at 192 kHz (3.3 MB and 12 MB in 1.0.1).
