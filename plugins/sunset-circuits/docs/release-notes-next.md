# Sunset Circuits, next release

Not released yet. The version number is set when this release is made.

- Analog drift is the same at every Oversampling setting and every session sample rate. The slow random pitch wander that the Analog control adds to each voice kept its depth at every setting, but it moved twice as fast at 4x as at 2x and half as fast at 1x. It now always moves the way it did at 2x in a 48 kHz session. Patches at 2x in a 48 kHz session sound exactly as before. At 1x the wander is now about twice as fast as it was, at 4x about half as fast, and at 2x in a 44.1 kHz session about 9 percent faster. Its depth does not change. Factory presets keep Analog low (0.2 to 0.35), so on most of them the difference is slight; the three 4x presets added in this release run Analog at full.
- Hard sync no longer adds aliasing. Each restart of the synced oscillator used to happen up to one internal sample early and as an abrupt jump. On bright and high notes that folded harsh, unrelated tones back into the audible range, worst when the master oscillator's pitch moves, for example with vibrato on the mod wheel. The restart now lands exactly where the master wraps and is smoothed the same way the oscillators' own edges are, so a synced sound aliases about as little as the same sound without sync. On a synced note two octaves above middle C at the default 2x, the strongest of those tones drops from 25 to 32 dB below the note to 56 dB or more below it.
- Oracle's sync now runs the other way round: oscillator 2 restarts oscillator 1, as on the instrument Oracle is modeled on. Oscillator 1 is the one Poly-Mod plays, so with Sync on, Poly-Mod and pitch modulation on oscillator 1 now sweep its timbre while the note keeps oscillator 2's pitch. Mono and Modular are unchanged (oscillator 1 restarts oscillator 2).
- Your own Oracle patches with Sync on will sound different: the oscillator that used to be restarted now does the restarting. If oscillator 1 and oscillator 2 are set the same, moving any pitch route from Osc1 Pitch to Osc2 Pitch gets the old sound back; otherwise swap the two oscillators' settings as well. The factory preset Oracle Trumpet was adjusted that way (its vibrato route moved to Osc2 Pitch); with the mod wheel up it differs slightly, because the poly-mod route to the filter always reads oscillator 2, which is now the master. The other factory presets that use sync are Mono and Modular presets and keep their sound, within 0.02 dB in level.
- Oracle's Poly-Mod routes now follow measurements of the instrument Oracle is modeled on. Filter envelope to oscillator 1 pitch raises it by a fixed musical interval (up to 34.7 semitones), the same on every note, where it used to add a fixed number of hertz (so it bent low notes much more than high ones). Oscillator 2 to oscillator 1 pitch bends by up to 7.1 semitones either way, where it used to apply a much stronger, linear FM. Filter envelope to pulse width now narrows the pulse, and can close it, where it used to widen it. Oscillator 2 to pulse width swings the pulse about twice as far as before, as measured, and can close it too. The two filter routes keep their character with new depths: up to 14.3 octaves from the filter envelope (was 4) and 3.1 octaves either way from oscillator 2 (was 3).
- Your own Oracle patches that use Poly-Mod will sound different. Filter envelope to filter needs about 0.28 times its old setting, and oscillator 2 to filter about 0.97 times, to sound as before. The pitch routes cannot be converted exactly, because a fixed interval and a fixed number of hertz only agree on one note; filter envelope to pulse width now works the other way round. The factory presets that use Poly-Mod (Brass Section, Poly Mod Bells, Dark Oracle, Stab Machine, Poly Brass, Glass Bells, Regal Brass and Oracle Trumpet) were adjusted: the filter routes exactly, the pitch routes as close as the new laws allow. Poly Mod Bells and Glass Bells keep their clang: their oscillator 2 to oscillator 1 depth was far beyond what the new law reaches even at full, so they now take it from Cross Mod, the old, stronger oscillator 2 to oscillator 1 modulation. Oracle's panel does not show Cross Mod; to bring that clang into a patch of your own, start from one of these two presets.
- Oracle's noise is now white, as on the instrument Oracle is modeled on, and set to that instrument's level beside its oscillators. It used to be pink. Turning the noise up also no longer turns the oscillators down: the noise used to count towards the mixer's level sum, so at full noise one oscillator at full dropped by about 3.5 dB and two by about 2 dB. The noise level is now the same at every Oversampling setting and every session sample rate.
- Your own Oracle patches that use noise will sound different. Next to the oscillators the new noise is far quieter in the bass (about 19 dB less at 31 Hz), about 4.5 dB quieter at 1 kHz, louder from 4 kHz up (about 1.5 dB at 4 kHz, 4 dB at 8 kHz), and about 2 dB quieter overall, and the oscillators no longer dip when it comes up. No factory preset uses Oracle's noise, so the factory bank sounds exactly as before. Noise in the other modes is unchanged.
- Mono and Modular no longer turn their oscillators down when the noise comes up. The noise used to count towards the mixer's level sum there too, so with a single oscillator at full and the noise at full the oscillator dropped by about 3 dB; now it stays put. The noise itself is still pink. Your own Mono and Modular patches that use noise get louder wherever their levels, noise included, added up to more than full, because nothing is turned down for the noise any more. The four factory presets that were affected (Sub Thunder, Alien Transmission, Noise Sweep and Modular English Horn) had their levels adjusted and sound as before.
- The filter that Cosmos, Oracle, Prism, Mono and Modular share costs less CPU, with no change to the sound (the output differs from before by less than -109 dB). A whole Oracle voice is about 7 to 8 percent cheaper.
- Oracle aliases much less at 2x and 4x. Its filter used to react to oscillator content far above hearing, which exists inside the plugin only because it runs at a higher internal rate, and that folded harsh, unrelated tones down into the audible range. The oscillators now pass through a steep low-pass at 18 kHz before the filter (a gentler one at 4x, which keeps the timing the same at every Oversampling setting), so a bright Oracle saw at the default 2x folds back about 68 dB below the note instead of about 47 dB. With the filter wide open the very top is slightly softer than before: 0.7 dB at 15 kHz, 1.4 dB at 16.7 kHz and 3 dB at 18.8 kHz. At 1x nothing changes.
- Oracle's oscillator 2 to filter route no longer folds back harsh tones on high notes. Oscillator 2 moves the cutoff at audio rate, and its highest harmonics used to spread the sound into sidebands beyond what the plugin can hold, which folded back down; the route now ignores oscillator 2's content above 10 kHz. On a C7 at the default 2x the fold-back drops from about 49 to 67 dB below the note. The growl of the route comes from oscillator 2's lower harmonics and is unchanged; Oracle Trumpet, the only factory preset with this route, changes by a hair (0.13 dB in the spectral comparison).
- The voices are laid out in memory so the processor handles them more efficiently, and CPU use no longer jumps by several percent from one version to the next for no audible reason. No change to the sound.
- Mono and Modular alias much less at 2x and 4x, for the same reason as Oracle and with the same cure: their oscillators pass through the same low-pass before the filter, and Modular's oscillator 3 to filter route ignores oscillator 3's content above 10 kHz. A bright saw at the default 2x folds back about 68 dB below the note instead of about 42 to 44 dB. Their factory presets sound the same (the largest change in the spectral comparison is 0.02 dB).
- The oscillators in Cosmos, Oracle, Mono and Modular alias far less at 1x and 2x: every edge of their waveforms, and every hard-sync restart, is now smoothed with a minimum-phase step instead of the two-sample correction they used before. A bright saw on its own folds back about 75 dB below the note at 1x (was about 35 dB) and at 2x (was about 56 dB). Oracle at the default 2x now aliases less than the instrument it is modeled on, on every row we measured. Very narrow pulses (the Poly-Mod routes can close a pulse) stay clean too. Factory presets sound the same (largest change in the spectral comparison 0.4 dB, on Poly Mod Bells and Glass Bells).
