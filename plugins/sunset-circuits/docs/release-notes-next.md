# Sunset Circuits, next release

## Opening an older project

A project recalls its program first, then restores the knob values you saved. This preserves your edits. It also means a project saved with a factory sound uses its old settings with the updated circuits. To hear the revised factory sound, select the preset again. Save a copy first if you want to keep your edits.

In migration tests, Poly Brass played 1.09 dB louder and sounded brighter with the old settings. Brass Section gained 0.44 dB, Poly Mod Bells lost its metallic attack, and Noise Sweep gained 2.52 dB. Your own patches can also change with Oracle's new sync direction, Poly-Mod response, white noise and deeper bass, the louder Mono and Modular noise mixes, or the new oversampling rates.

## Oracle

- **Sync.** Oscillator 2 now restarts oscillator 1. For vibrato on a synced patch, route it to oscillator 2. Modulating oscillator 1 sweeps the tone while oscillator 2 holds the note. Oscillator 1 has no independent semitone control, so an older sync patch can need retuning by ear. Oracle Trumpet now routes its vibrato to oscillator 2. In Mono and Modular, oscillator 1 still restarts oscillator 2.
- **Poly-Mod.** The filter envelope raises oscillator 1 by the same musical interval on any note, up to 33.9 semitones. Oscillator 2 can bend it by up to 7.1 semitones each way. The envelope now narrows oscillator 1's pulse. Oscillator 2 swings its width about twice as far as before. Either route can close the pulse. The two cutoff routes reach 14.3 octaves from the envelope and 3.1 octaves either way from oscillator 2. To approximate an older filter sweep, start at 28 percent of its previous envelope amount or 97 percent of its oscillator 2 amount. The old pitch settings have no single equivalent across the keyboard.
- **Cross Mod.** The Poly-Mod panel now has a separate knob for linear frequency modulation. The earlier bell voicings used 50 percent on Poly Mod Bells and 60 percent on Glass Bells. Dark Oracle, Stab Machine, Regal Brass and Oracle Trumpet also have revised Poly-Mod settings.
- **Brass and bell presets.** Poly Brass plays an octave below the key with a short release. Brass Section swells into a stereo room. Poly Mod Bells has a low, centered ring and a long release. Glass Bells strikes an octave up with a higher overtone and a stereo tail.
- **Chord playback.** When several Oracle notes arrive on the same sample, they can now take separate voices while older notes are held or releasing. The five-voice limit and its unison reduction still apply.
- **Noise and bass.** Oracle now mixes in white noise without turning down the oscillators. Against the old pink noise at the same setting, the noise measured about 19 dB lower at 31 Hz, 4.5 dB lower at 1 kHz and 4 dB higher at 8 kHz. Noise level stays consistent across sample rates. Separately, the filter and stereo output now pass more low bass and still remove DC. In a pulse-closure test the tail fell to about 37 percent of its starting level in 31 ms. Bass-heavy patches can produce higher peaks.
- **Wheel Mod.** Open WHEEL MOD beside MOD MATRIX. One free-running LFO and one noise source move all voices together. Mix saw, triangle or square shapes with noise or one random step per cycle. The wheel moves between your lower and upper depth limits. Switch on any combination of oscillator 1 or 2 pitch, oscillator 1 or 2 pulse width, and filter cutoff. All five destinations start off. Older projects open with Wheel Mod off.
- **Front panel.** The black Oracle panel has white outlines and labels, red lamps and a walnut frame. Routing arrows now display their direction, including OSC 2 -> OSC 1 under Sync.

## Prism

- **Operator levels.** Each operator's Level now follows the output-level law of the mid-80s four-operator instruments Prism is modeled on: each percent of the knob is about 0.75 dB, so the knob covers about 74 dB instead of 48. A modulator at full level also modulates as deep as on that hardware, half as deep as before. In a comparison with a four-operator reference, Prism's level steps now land within 0.7 dB of it from 40 percent up, and its modulation depth within 0.2 dB from 70 to 81 percent and within 0.7 dB from 36 percent. The factory sounds were converted and keep their sound on middle C. Glass Keys and Glass Cathedral sound different away from middle C, because their Key Scale works through the new law. A Prism patch saved with an older version opens with the same knob values, so its operators below full play quieter or modulate less: a carrier at 50 percent plays about 13 dB lower, and a modulator at 50 percent gives about a ninth of its old depth.
- **Feedback.** Operator 4's seven feedback steps now have the strengths of the hardware Prism is modeled on, and the feedback follows operator 4's own level and envelope as it does there: a quieter or decaying operator 4 feeds back less, so its edge fades with the note. The top step is now a smooth, saw-like tone; before, the two highest steps lost level and turned harsh. In the comparison, each step's harmonics land within 1.3 dB rms of the reference, and the second harmonic follows operator level and envelope within 0.4 dB. Solid Bass and Brass Machine now use the top step. Solid Bass sounds as before; Brass Machine's sustain is brighter. In older patches every step is weaker than it was, by 6 to 11 dB at full operator level.
- **Clean tone.** Prism's filter no longer adds a slight distortion of its own. A single operator at full level used to carry a third harmonic at -54 dB; it is now at -88 dB, as on the reference. The factory sounds change by half a decibel at most, in the attack of Prism Talking Keys on low notes.
- **Key Scale.** Each operator's Key Scale now follows the key level scaling of the hardware: turned to the left it takes level off toward the high keys, about 6 dB at middle C at full scaling and doubling every octave up, on carriers and modulators alike. Turned to the right it mirrors that toward the low keys. Before, it multiplied the level, so it also raised the other side of middle C. In the comparison it lands within 0.01 dB of the reference at moderate settings and within 3 dB at full scaling on the top keys. Glass Keys and Glass Cathedral were converted and keep their sound from middle C up; Glass Keys' tine is softer on the lowest notes. In older patches Key Scale works in the new way.
- **Operator attacks.** Each operator's attack now rises the way the hardware's does: quickly out of silence, then easing into full level, where before it started slowly and sped up toward the end. In the comparison its shape lands within 0.03 of the reference across the rise from -40 dB to full. The factory sounds with slow operator attacks were converted to keep their swell (Brass Machine, Glass Cathedral, Prism Talking Keys); their first 50 ms come out up to 2 dB louder. In older patches a slow attack swells in sooner; lengthen it by about 30 percent for the old feel.
- **Operator decay and sustain.** Each operator's Sustain now follows the hardware's sustain law, about 3 dB per fifteenth of the knob over 45 dB, so its lower half is usable instead of crowded near silence. The decay falls in a straight line in decibels and stops at the sustain level, as on the hardware, where before it slowed as it approached it. The factory sounds were converted and keep their sustained level exactly; their decays come out within about 1 dB, and Prism Talking Keys within 2.7 dB in its first 200 ms. In older patches a Sustain below full sits lower: 50 percent was -6 dB and is now about -23 dB.
- **Ratios.** An operator Ratio typed in or set with Shift is now kept to a fine resolution, so the hardware's inharmonic ratios play in tune: in the comparison each of the reference's ratios lands within 0.4 cents, where the earlier resolution put some up to 12 cents off. Factory sounds are unchanged. A ratio in an older patch that fell between the old steps can shift by a few cents. Dragging the Ratio knob or turning the mouse wheel over it now steps through the hardware's ratio table as well as the classic ratios, 53 values in order, so 1.41, 1.57 and 1.73 times each whole ratio no longer need typing. Each step takes 5 pixels of drag, the same across the whole knob. The wheel now moves to the next value above or below a typed ratio. Shift still drags continuously.
- **Second decay (D2).** Each operator has a second decay, as on the hardware: once the decay reaches the sustain level, the operator keeps falling at its own rate, 60 dB in the time shown, down to silence while the key is held. It runs on the same rates as Decay, up to 86 seconds. At the top of the knob, HOLD, the sustain level holds as before, so older patches and the factory sounds are unchanged by it. In the comparison its slope lands within 0.6 percent of the time shown, at every oversampling rate.
- **Rate scaling (KEY RT).** Each operator's envelope, attack, decay, D2 and release, now runs faster on higher keys, as on the hardware. KEY RT 0 to 3 sets how much: each step doubles the tracking, and at 3 the envelope runs twice as fast every octave. At 0 it tracks slightly, as the hardware does at its lowest setting: one step slower (about 1.2 times) at C2 and below, unchanged from C#2 to C4, one step faster from C#4 to C6 and two steps (about 1.4 times) from C#6 up. The times on the knobs are exact at middle C. In the comparison each setting lands within 0.4 of the hardware's rate steps from C1 to C7. Older Prism patches open at KEY RT 0, so their envelopes now run about 1.2 times faster from C#4 to C6, about 1.4 times faster from C#6 up, and about 1.2 times slower at C2 and below; from C#2 to C4 they are unchanged. The six Prism factory sounds change the same way.
- **Operator strip.** Each operator strip now has six columns: RATIO, FINE, LEVEL, VEL, KEY and KEY RT above, A, D, S, D2 and R below, with operator 4's FB at the end of its lower row.

## Oscillators, noise and processing

Cosmos, Oracle, Mono and Modular use minimum-phase smoothing on oscillator edges at lower internal rates. Hard-sync restarts also land at the point within a sample where the master wraps. With Oracle playing a steady C7 (MIDI 96, three octaves above middle C) at 2x and 48 kHz, the strongest unwanted tone measured between -109.0 and -91.7 dBc across five waveforms. On Oracle Trumpet with the wheel up, a different test measured energy outside the moving harmonics at -66.6, -69.2 and -71.0 dB on C6, G6 and C7.

Oracle, Mono and Modular also filter the oscillator mix before the voice filter. The pre-filter uses four poles at 18 kHz from an internal rate of 88.2 kHz and two poles at 20 kHz from 176.4 kHz. With the filter wide open, the earlier 18 kHz comparison measured 0.7 dB less at 15 kHz and 3 dB less at 18.8 kHz. Oracle's oscillator 2 and Modular's oscillator 3 cutoff routes also limit the highest harmonics driving the filter. The shared voice filter takes less CPU, and the voices are laid out more efficiently in memory.

Mono and Modular keep pink noise. Raising it now leaves the oscillators at their set levels, so older mixes with noise can play louder. Sub Thunder, Alien Transmission, Noise Sweep and Modular English Horn have adjusted factory levels.

Oversampling now targets an internal rate. At 44.1 or 48 kHz, 1x, 2x and 4x still multiply the session rate. At 88.2 or 96 kHz, 1x and 2x both run at the session rate and 4x runs at twice that rate. Older projects at these rates can sound different at the same setting. At 176.4 or 192 kHz, all three run at the session rate. Reported latency stays at 13 host samples in every mode and setting.

The Analog control's slow pitch drift keeps its depth and follows the speed it had at 2x in a 48 kHz session. At 48 kHz the wander is about twice as fast as before at 1x and half as fast at 4x.

## Level across modes

All six modes now play at Cosmos's level, so switching modes no longer jumps the volume. Before, Oracle and Acid played 5 to 6 dB louder than Cosmos on the same patch, and Mono, Modular and Prism about 4 dB louder. Cosmos is unchanged. On the factory starting patch, a held note in any mode now measures on average within 0.1 dB of Cosmos across C2 to C5, and chords within 0.2 dB, except on Modular, where they play about 2.4 dB quieter. The match is applied after the effects, so Drive, delay and reverb sound as before. It also holds on a new instance: Oscillator 1 and 2 Level now default to 0.8 and 0.6, the values the factory sounds start from, instead of 1.0 and 0.8, at which a new instance played Cosmos about 2 dB above the other modes. Projects and presets keep their saved levels; only a new instance and a reset to default change.

The factory presets were leveled to one loudness with their Master Volume, so switching presets, in any mode, no longer jumps the volume. Each plays about as loud as Init Cosmos's chord when it is played the way it is meant to be: a pad as a chord, a lead or bass as a line, an arpeggio or an Acid pattern as it runs. Forty-nine of the 57 land within 0.2 dB of that level, and Poly Mod Bells 0.6 dB under it so that its highest chords also peak below -1 dBFS. Before, presets played this way spread over about 16 dB; the Cosmos arpeggios sat 8 to 13 dB under the pads, some basses and drones up to 12 dB under, and a few leads up to 3 dB above. The largest changes, counting the mode level: Glass Highway is 12.8 dB louder, Warm Keys 11.9 dB, Crystal Rain 10.8 dB, Acid Machine 10.5 dB, Noise Sweep 10.3 dB and Brass Section 9.3 dB, and Analog Strings is 3.7 dB quieter, Brass Machine 3.3 dB, Fat Fifth 3.2 dB and Screaming Lead 3.1 dB. Seven presets have strong attacks and stop where their peaks reach -1 dBFS. Judged by their loudest moment, Glass Keys, Glass Bells, Warm Keys, Crystal Rain and Prism Talking Keys come within 2 dB of the rest, Acid Squelch about 2.5 dB under, and Stab Machine about 5 dB under.

Master Volume now reaches +18 dB, up from +6 dB, so that the quietest sounds can reach the bank's level. Projects and presets keep their Master Volume setting. In a VST3 host, existing automation of Master Volume moves, because VST3 automation stores a position on the knob rather than a level: a point that was at 0 dB now plays at about +10.9 dB. Redraw Master Volume automation in VST3 projects. CLAP, LV2 and AU automation keeps its levels.

Your own patches in Oracle, Mono, Modular, Prism and Acid play quieter, by 5.1, 4.9, 3.8, 3.7 and 5.8 dB. Raise Master Volume by that amount to get the old level back. A project saved with a factory preset in one of those modes restores the preset's old Master Volume, so it plays quieter too. Select the preset again to load its new level.

## Factory presets

The bank now has 57 sounds. Oracle Trumpet is a hard-synced brass lead, Modular English Horn is a reedy lead, and Prism Talking Keys is a four-operator FM patch with a vowel-like attack. Prism Talking Keys is the final name for the patch originally called Prism Talking Clav. All three retain their original 4x setting. All three are by ZULU MATRIX. In the preset menu, each one sits under a ZULU MATRIX heading at the foot of its engine's column. The browser lists them under a new ARTISTS bank chip with the author under each name, and its search now matches author names.

## Preset arrows

The previous and next arrows now step through the presets in the preset menu's order, column by column. They used to follow the bank's internal order, which jumps between engines: from Stab Machine, next loaded Pulsing Darkness, a Mono preset, instead of Upside Down. MIDI program numbers and host program lists keep the bank order.
